A daily loss brake is a hard limit on how much you can lose in one day. When you hit it, you stop for the day: no "one more trade to win it back".

How it is usually written

  • Pick a fixed amount or a small percentage of the balance you set aside for trading.
  • Count realised losses and fees. On 1% terminals, fees alone add up quickly over many trades.
  • When the limit is reached, stop trading until the next day. Some people also move funds out of the hot trading wallet as a physical brake.

Why it matters

  • Tilt is real. After losses, people tend to take bigger and faster risks to recover. A brake caps the damage from a bad day.
  • Fees compound. A day of many small trades can lose money even if prices go nowhere, because each round trip pays fees twice.
  • It keeps you in the learning game. Small, limited losses leave room to review what happened.

Making it stick

  • Write the number down before the session starts.
  • Use a separate trading wallet with only the amount you are prepared to lose.
  • Keep a simple log: time, tool, size, result, fee. Review it after you stop, not during.
