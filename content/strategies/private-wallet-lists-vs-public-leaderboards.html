Scanners such as GMGN show public leaderboards of "smart" or top-earning wallets. Some traders instead keep a private list of wallets they have reviewed themselves. This page explains the difference as a question of signal quality and risk.

Public leaderboards

  • Everyone sees the same list. When thousands of people watch and copy the same wallets, the copies arrive late and can move the price against each other.
  • Past results are not a forecast. A leaderboard ranks what already happened over a window; it says little about how a wallet will do next.
  • Wallets can be gamed. A wallet can look profitable because of insider allocations, wash trades, or because its owner knows a public audience will follow it.
  • Copy-trading caveats. Trojan's own FAQ notes that good copy-trading wallets "are highly valued and rarely shared publicly", and warns against copying snipers and wallets using custom programs.

Private lists

  • You choose the criteria. For example: how long the wallet has been active, whether it holds through drawdowns, whether it trades sizes similar to yours.
  • Fewer copiers. A list nobody else follows is less likely to be crowded, but it is also only as good as your review.
  • Maintenance. Wallets change behaviour, get abandoned, or rotate to new addresses. A private list needs regular pruning.

Risk-management takeaways

  • Treat any wallet list (public or private) as one input to research, never as an instruction.
  • Verify on an explorer such as Solscan what a wallet actually did, including fees paid.
  • Remember fees: on a 1% terminal, every copied round trip costs about 2% before price moves (see the compare pages).
