The "5-minute survival hold" is a self-imposed waiting rule: when a token has just launched, you do nothing for a set time (five minutes is just a round example) and only then look at it. It is a risk-management habit, not a way to find winners.

Why people use a waiting rule

  • The first minutes are the most chaotic. On a bonding curve every buy moves the price up and every sell moves it down (see reading a pump.fun bonding curve). Early prices can swing hard on a handful of wallets.
  • Some risks show up quickly. A creator or a cluster of linked wallets dumping, an authority that was never revoked, or holder concentration that a checker flags are often visible within minutes.
  • It slows down impulse. A fixed wait breaks the "fear of missing out" loop and gives you time to run checks.

What to do during the wait (a checklist, not a signal)

  1. Open a safety checker such as RugCheck or Solsniffer and read the individual flags, not just the score.
  2. Look at the holder map in Bubblemaps for connected wallets.
  3. Note whether the token is still on its bonding curve or has graduated to a pool.
  4. Check how many wallets bought in the very first block (see how bundles work).

What the wait cannot do

  • Surviving five minutes does not mean a token is safe. Many tokens fall after the first minutes, and authorities or holder positions can change later.
  • Waiting means you will often see a higher (or lower) price than the first buyers did. That is the trade-off you are choosing on purpose.
  • Five minutes is an example. Some people use longer waits; the point is to have a rule and follow it.
Framing: this is a way to reduce avoidable mistakes, not a strategy for making money. Most new tokens lose most of their value.
