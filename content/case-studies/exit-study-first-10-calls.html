The first ten paper calls ended 3 wins, 7 losses, average -24.0% net after an estimated 2.5% round-trip cost. This study re-runs all ten from pump.fun trade data, at the prices that actually traded, to ask two questions: would a different exit rule have done better, and how well did the stops really work? Ten calls is a tiny sample, so nothing here is proven.

Paper calls only · not advice · data as of 2:10 AM IST, Oct 23, 2026

The final record

  • 3 wins (Arnaud, DIF, ship), 7 losses, average -24.0% net per call, about -8.0R in total. Running totals are on the paper scoreboard.
  • ship closed as a win at +23.7%. Half was sold at 2x. It went on to peak at +305%, then one sell gapped the rest from about $82k to $17.9k, far below its stop.
  • Half the coins ran past 2x at some point after the call. The losses did not come from coins that never moved.

  • The trailing stop was worst. A 40% trail after +50% sounds like it lets winners run, but gaps blew straight through it: php went from +11.0% to -75.4% and DIF from +32.3% to -26.5%. Only ship did better (+127.4%). Rejected.
  • The 15-minute time stop was slightly better (-19.1% vs -20.8%), but the whole difference came from one coin (MINE, -15.4% instead of -32.6%). Testing, logged alongside the "no clock exit while buys lead" rule.
  • The current rules stay: -30% stop and half at 2x. All three wins came from that 2x half.

The entry price was wrong on three calls

For Arnaud, FORM and Pumuckel, the market cap written down at the call was 16 to 20%+ above anything that actually traded around it. FORM's last trade before the call was about $15.8k against an $18.8k call; Pumuckel's was about $40.4k against $47.6k. Arnaud's first trade after the call was already below its stop, so it was scored as an instant loss.

The pullback-then-reclaim entry: rejected

One idea was to wait for a 20-40% dip in the first 15 minutes and enter only when the price made a new high. 0 of 10 coins showed that pattern (Arnaud's apparent match was the bad entry price above), so the rule would never have fired.

Three kinds of coin

  • Fast flips: spike, then one-sell dumps within minutes.
  • Slow builders: dip, then run 40 to 90 minutes later. GRAIL was stopped at 10:18 IST, then peaked at +109% at 11:46 IST.
  • Dead on arrival: never above +25% after the call. YAI, EKO, MINE and Pumuckel.

The catch is that the type is only known afterward. Whether any early signal predicts it is now being tested; until then, it cannot be used to pick an exit.

What changed in the rulebook

  • Adopted: entry = first real trade after the call; log the peak, the low before the peak and the real stop fill; judge rules on average R, not win rate.
  • Testing: the 15-minute time stop; shadow skips (WOULD-SKIP) for 30%+ same-slot buys, platform-token curves and launchpad stories; RugCheck's largest insider network at 10%+ of supply. Extraction moved from a kill to log-only (see testing free rug-check tools).
  • Rejected: the 40% trailing stop after +50%; the pullback-reclaim entry.
  • Kept: -30% stop, half at 2x, the daily brake, and the kill on bundle or linked-wallet dumps.

Every rule's status and evidence is on the desk rulebook and in rules.json.

A post-mortem of paper calls, not a recommendation about any coin named here. Ten calls cannot prove a rule works, and the 2.5% cost is an estimate. Most new tokens lose most of their value.
