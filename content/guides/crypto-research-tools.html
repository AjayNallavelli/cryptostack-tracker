Dollar figures in that post’s image were not copied: several were out of date or incomplete when checked against official pages on Nov 10, 2026. Where we could not verify a price, the access column is left blank.

Education only. Not financial advice. Crypto is high risk. Nothing here is a coin pick, a signal or a reason to buy or sell. A fundraising record, a labelled wallet, a social spike or an AI summary is not due diligence on its own.

How to use these together

This is a way to read information, not a trading system and not a sequence that implies a buy or a sell.

  1. Get context, not a target. CoinGecko or CoinMarketCap can show how a market is usually quoted. DefiLlama, Token Terminal, Dune, Artemis or Glassnode can show protocol or asset activity over time. Use them to understand what a number means, not to decide that something is cheap or expensive.
  2. Read the actual market, not only a “trending” list. DEX Screener is for pair pages and charts; TradingView is for broader technical charting; Revert is for LP-position maths. A trending rank can be paid-for or short-lived.
  3. Ask who holds the supply, then doubt the labels. Bubblemaps can show clustered holders. Arkham and Nansen attach names to wallets; InsightX flags wallets it associates with a deployer. Those tags can be wrong, incomplete or out of date. For Solana mint/freeze authority, LP locks and holder concentration, see Spotting rugs.
  4. Read the contract path when you need to. Tenderly and BlockSec’s Phalcon Explorer help you see what a transaction would do. A clean simulation is not an audit and does not mean a token is safe.
  5. Treat fundraising and “early” social tools as archives. CryptoRank, RootData, ICO Analytics and Crypto Fundraising record past raises. AlphaGate watches a curated set of accounts. None of that tells you what a token will do after launch. Whales Market is a pre-listing venue; pre-market prices can be thin and are easy to misread.
  6. Treat social feeds and AI answers as leads to check. Moni, Dexu AI and CryptoPanic aggregate public posts and headlines. Surf AI can speed up reading but still needs a check against an explorer, a vendor’s own docs or a primary filing. Do not take a chatbot summary as a fact.
  7. Keep portfolio tools separate from research. Zapper and NFTScan are useful for seeing what an address holds. You do not need to connect a wallet to learn how a tool works, and you should never paste a seed phrase into any of these sites.

You do not need all 31 tools. Most of the work is a price page, a pair chart, a holder view and a way to read a contract — plus the habit of writing down what would prove you wrong.
