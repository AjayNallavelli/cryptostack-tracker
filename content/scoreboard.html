Notes

  • Every entry is a paper call, not advice. No coin here is a recommendation, and past paper results say nothing about future ones.
  • Entry price = market cap when the call was named ÷ 1 billion supply. Exits follow the written exit line: -30% stop, or half at 2x with the rest on the same stop.
  • Stops are filled on paper at exactly -30%. In fast crashes the real price can gap far below it: Pumuckel's first trade through its stop was about -67%.
  • php and YAI were named before the exit rules existed and were marked to the floor. Re-reading their full trade history: php briefly touched 2x (about 9 minutes after the call) before collapsing, and YAI would have hit its stop about 2 minutes after the call. With today's rules applied to both, calls 1-6 average about -10.8% instead of -38.6%. The logged results stand.
  • Earlier versions of this table showed R as gross ÷ 30 (+1.17 / -1.0). It is now net ÷ 30 throughout (+1.08 / -1.08).
  • The 2.5% round-trip cost is an estimate, applied equally to every call. Real fees, priority fees and slippage can be higher, especially in thin pools.
  • ship is open and marked at its last check; it can still change in either direction.
  • 10 calls is a tiny sample. What these calls do and don't show is written up in the first 10 paper calls.
