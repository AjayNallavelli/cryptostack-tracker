What this calculator does

It splits a completed trade into gross profit or loss, estimated fees, net profit or loss, and a breakeven exit price. Gross P/L is the price change times quantity (reversed for a short). Fees are the entry-fee percentage on the entry notional, the exit-fee percentage on the exit notional, and any flat fees you add. Net P/L is gross minus fees. The breakeven price is the exit at which net P/L would be zero.

Pair it with the trading fee calculator to pick a fee rate, and with stop-loss and take-profit ladders to see how a planned exit looks after costs.

Example (labelled as an example)

This is a worked example, not a trade and not advice. A long of 1 unit, entry 100, exit 110, and 0.10% fees each way: gross P/L is +10, fees are 0.10 + 0.11 = 0.21, and net P/L is +9.79 (about +9.79% of the $100 entry value). Breakeven is 100.2002, a little above 100, because both the entry and the exit still pay a fee if you close flat. The same prices as a short would be a net loss: gross −10, plus the same 0.21 of fees.

How to read the result

  • A small win can be a net loss once fees are included, especially at small size or with a 1% terminal fee on Solana.
  • Breakeven for a long sits above entry; for a short it sits below entry, as long as fees are positive.
  • Funding, borrow interest and slippage are not included. For a round-trip fee only, use the fee calculator.

FAQ

Should the exit fee use the exit price or the entry price?

This calculator applies the exit fee to the exit notional (exit price × quantity), which is how most venues charge a close. If your venue charges both sides on the entry notional, type the rates accordingly or use the fee calculator on a fixed size.

Can I model a DEX priority fee?

Yes, put the priority fee and any tip into the flat-fees box, in the same unit as price × quantity. Percentage venue or curve fees go in the entry and exit fee fields.

Is a positive net P/L a reason to take a trade?

No. This is backward-looking arithmetic on numbers you already typed. It does not forecast price or recommend a trade.
