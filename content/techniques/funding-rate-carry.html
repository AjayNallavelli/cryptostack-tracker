How it works

Perpetual futures usually have a funding rate: periodic payments between longs and shorts that tug the perp price toward the spot index. Official venue docs describe the formula, the interval and who pays. On Hyperliquid’s perp venue (HyperCore), funding is documented in the Hyperliquid funding pages. Centralized exchanges, including Binance (Referral link), publish their own funding rules for USD-M and coin-M contracts. Intervals and formulas are not the same everywhere — read the venue you would actually use.

A funding-rate carry trade tries to collect those payments while staying roughly hedged to price. One common shape:

  1. Hold a long (or short) perpetual that is receiving funding.
  2. Hedge the price risk with an opposite position in spot, a dated future, or another perp, so a move in the coin is mostly cancelled.
  3. Collect (or pay) funding on the schedule the venue uses, minus trading fees, borrow costs and basis changes.
  4. Watch margin on the derivatives leg. A “hedged” book can still be liquidated if one leg is leveraged and the other is not fully offset, or if marks diverge. See avoiding liquidation and the related spot–futures basis page.

If you only hold the perp and do not hedge, you are not running a carry trade. You are running a directional bet that also happens to pay or charge funding.

When it fails

  • The rate flips. A rate you were receiving can turn into a rate you pay, sometimes quickly around events.
  • The hedge is imperfect. Different indexes, different expiry, or a spot that cannot be sold or withdrawn when you need it leave residual price risk.
  • Fees and spreads eat the rate. A small positive funding number does not clear entry/exit costs if you have to reposition often.
  • Liquidation or auto-deleveraging on one leg. The other leg then becomes a naked directional position.
  • Venue and custody risk. The carry lives on the exchange or protocol that pays it.

Worked example

Example (not a recommendation, and not a current rate). Suppose a perp pays longs 0.01% every 8 hours, and a trader is long $10,000 of the perp and short $10,000 of the same asset in spot as a hedge. If the rate stays unchanged for 24 hours, gross funding received is 0.03% × $10,000 = $3 that day, before trading fees and any borrow cost on the short spot. If the mark price of the perp jumps and maintenance margin is breached on the long, the $10,000 short spot remains and the book is no longer hedged. If the rate flips to −0.05% for one interval, that interval costs $5, which can wipe several earlier intervals of carry.

Risks

  • Funding is variable. Historical average rates are not a promise.
  • Hedged carry still has operational, margin, and counterparty risk.
  • This page is education only. It is not a suggestion to run a carry trade or to use any venue.
