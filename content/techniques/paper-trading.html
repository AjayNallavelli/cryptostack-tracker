How it works

Paper trading is practice without (or before) putting meaningful money at risk: a testnet, a venue’s demo book, or a written log of hypothetical fills. The point is to test whether a method can be followed — entries, exits, sizing, and a daily loss brake — and to see where the process breaks. It is not a forecast that the same results will appear in a live account.

Published “paper then live” playbooks usually insist on:

  1. The same rules as live, including fees, funding, and a stop that you actually “place” in the log or on the demo venue. A paper book that assumes perfect fills is a different game.
  2. A journal of process vs outcome. See post-trade journaling.
  3. A minimum sample and a pre-written promotion test, so a short lucky streak is not treated as proof.
  4. Honesty about the gap between paper and live: real fills slip, demo liquidity can be fake, queues and MEV do not always show up, and emotions change when the money is real.
  5. Promotion needs evidence, not a narrative. If the paper log cannot show that stops were respected and that results survived fees, there is nothing to promote.

CryptoStack already keeps a public, delayed paper scoreboard and paper case studies for named research processes. Those pages are themselves education; they are not a claim that paper results will repeat, and they are not a signal to copy a trade.

When it fails

  • Fills are fantasized. “I would have got the low” is not a fill.
  • Fees, spreads, funding and failed transactions are skipped. The paper edge can vanish live, especially on small-size DEX routes.
  • The method changes every session, so nothing is actually tested.
  • A short window is treated as a statistically meaningful test.
  • Live size is much larger than paper size, so liquidity and psychology are new variables.

Worked example

Example (not a recommendation). A trader papers a 20-session grid on a liquid major, with $10,000 notional and a written abort if price leaves a $90–$110 range. The log records every child fill at the then-current bid or ask plus a 0.1% fee assumption, not at mid. After 20 sessions the paper book is +$180 after fees, but three of the “wins” required fills that a real $10,000 clip would have moved. The honest finding is “the method is unproven at this size,” not “it made 1.8%.” A pre-written promotion rule that required, say, 40 sessions and a fee-adjusted result above zero after a realistic slippage haircut would refuse promotion. That refusal is the method working.

Risks

  • Paper trading can create overconfidence. Demo books can be badly calibrated. Testnet tokens are not mainnet value.
  • Some “demo” sites are phishing. Use official venue docs and official testnets only.
  • This page is education only. It is not a suggestion to start a live account after any paper streak.
