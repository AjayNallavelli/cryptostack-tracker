What Monad is

Official docs describe Monad as a high-performance, EVM-compatible layer 1. Public mainnet is dated 24 November 2025. The mainnet chain ID is 143. Because it is EVM-compatible, familiar Ethereum tooling can often be pointed at Monad — but it is a different network, with a different gas token (MON, not ETH), a different consensus mechanism (official docs call it MonadBFT) and a shorter production history than Ethereum or Solana. Always confirm chain ID 143 from official docs before signing anything.

Verified official links (website, explorers, social accounts) are collected on Monad’s official links page. Use that list, not a search ad or a chat message, if you need a URL.

Gas token, fees and speed

The native gas token is MON. Fees follow an Ethereum-style gas model on a different coin and a different fee market. This page does not quote a USD transaction cost; any such figure would go stale and is not a substitute for a live gas tracker.

Official network documentation (checked 10 Nov 2026) states:

  • 300 millisecond block times.
  • 600 millisecond finality, described as two slots.
  • A block gas budget of 150 million gas, which official materials also express as about 500 million gas per second.
  • A throughput capacity figure of 10,000+ transactions per second. That is a stated capacity, not a measurement of live demand on any given day.

Official explorers

Official docs and the official links page point to two public explorers: monadvision.com (MonadVision) and monadscan.com (Monadscan). You can look up a transaction, an address or a token without connecting a wallet. Bookmark those official URLs yourself. Do not trust an explorer, RPC or “support” link from a DM, a comment reply or a look-alike domain, and never paste a seed phrase or private key into a website.

Bridging basics

This is a description of official documentation, not an instruction to move funds. If you do not need an asset on Monad, you do not need a bridge.

Official Tokens and Bridges docs list several third-party routes rather than a single Monad-operated canonical withdrawal window of the Layer-2 type. As of the 10 Nov 2026 check, those docs mention LayerZero (via Stargate), Chainlink CCIP, Hyperlane (via Nexus), a 2/2 NTT MonadBridge path, and an aggregator (Jumper). Each route has its own trust assumptions, fees and delays. This page does not rank them, does not publish contract addresses, and does not tell you to use any of them.

If you ever use a bridge, start from the official tokens-and-bridges page and the official links list, confirm chain ID 143, double-check the domain, and treat unsolicited “bridge” links and fake RPCs as phishing. A wrong-network transfer can permanently lose funds.

How to evaluate a token on Monad

This is a reading list, not a trading method and not a reason to buy anything. Some screening tools have shorter history, or incomplete coverage, on a newer chain.

  • Read the contract, holders and transactions on MonadVision or Monadscan.
  • Look at the pool and recent trades on a public screener such as DEX Screener, if the pair is listed there.
  • Inspect holder clusters with Bubblemaps where the chain is covered.
  • Use contract-screening tools such as GoPlus and Token Sniffer only if they actually support Monad for that contract; a missing result is not a pass, and a “passed” badge is not a safety guarantee.
  • Read spotting rugs and crypto research tools for broader context.

None of those tools can make a token safe. They can only show you what is already on-chain.

Trading styles that fit Monad

This is a venue-fit note, not a recommendation to trade. Official docs describe a fast EVM L1 (300 ms blocks / 600 ms finality, MON for gas, mainnet from 24 Nov 2025). That speed is a mechanical match for short-horizon DEX activity in principle. A dated DefiLlama snapshot on 11 Nov 2026 (Asia/Calcutta) showed about $37M of 24-hour DEX volume — thinner, newer liquidity than Solana, BNB Chain, Ethereum or Base — so cautious DEX use, listed launchpads on the official App Hub, and paper-style sizing are a better description than “a primary memecoin venue.” App Hub listings are directory facts, not endorsements. The 10k+ TPS figure is capacity, not a promise of filled trades. See the memecoin risk checklist, position sizing and trading styles by chain.

Risks

  • Newer network. Public mainnet is dated 24 November 2025. Shorter production history means less battle-testing of clients, tooling, bridges and apps than on long-running chains.
  • Capacity is not demand. Official TPS and gas-per-second figures describe designed capacity, not guaranteed inclusion quality or liquid markets.
  • New tokens are usually worthless. Launchpad listings on a new chain are especially noisy.
  • Phishing and fake official sites. Use only URLs on the official links page. Fake explorers, RPCs and “MON” airdrop sites are a predictable risk on a new L1.
  • Bridging risk. Multiple third-party routes means multiple trust models. A wrong chain ID (Monad is 143, not 1 or 56) can lose funds.

Nothing on this page is financial advice, a recommendation to hold MON, or a suggestion to launch or trade a token.
