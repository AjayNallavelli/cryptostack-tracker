What Solana is

Solana is a layer-1 network: transactions are executed and finalized on Solana itself, rather than being settled to another chain the way an Ethereum Layer 2 is. Official materials describe a proof-of-stake validator set, with Proof of History used as a timing mechanism. You do not need to run a validator, or to use any particular wallet, in order to read the chain. Explorers and public RPC endpoints are enough to inspect a transaction or a token mint.

Most activity that this site already documents — including pump.fun-style token launches and Solana DEX routing — happens on mainnet-beta, Solana’s production cluster. Test clusters are for development only and are not covered here.

Gas token, fees and speed

The native coin is SOL. Fees are denominated in lamports (1 SOL = 1,000,000,000 lamports).

Official fee docs (checked 10 Nov 2026) describe the current model this way:

  • Each transaction pays a base fee of 5,000 lamports (0.000005 SOL) per signature.
  • Half of that base fee is burned and half is paid to the validator that includes the transaction.
  • A sender may also attach an optional priority fee so validators are more likely to include the transaction when the network is busy. Priority fees vary with demand; there is no single official “typical USD fee.”
  • A transaction is limited to a maximum of 1.4 million compute units.

On 9 Oct 2026 (epoch 1053), official Solana upgrade notes say mainnet slot times were reduced from 400 milliseconds to 200 milliseconds (SIMD-0525). Older pages and third-party explainers that still say “400 ms slots” are out of date. A slot is a scheduling window, not a guarantee that every slot contains a confirmed transaction, and confirmation or finality can take more than one slot.

Official explorer

The official block explorer is explorer.solana.com. You can look up a transaction signature, an account, or a token mint without connecting a wallet. Solscan is a widely used independent explorer that this site already documents; it is a third-party product, not the official explorer. Bookmark official URLs yourself. Do not trust an “explorer” or “support” link from a DM, a comment reply or a look-alike domain, and never paste a seed phrase or private key into a website.

Bridging basics

This is a description of how assets typically move, not an instruction to move funds. If you do not need an asset on Solana, you do not need a bridge.

Solana is a layer 1, so there is no single official “canonical withdrawal” window of the kind Ethereum Layer 2s use. Moving assets between Solana and other networks usually means a third-party bridge or a messaging protocol. Each bridge has its own trust assumptions, fees, delays and failure modes. There is no official Solana Foundation portal that this page can point to as the one correct bridge.

If you ever use a bridge, start from a project’s official documentation, double-check the domain, confirm you selected the intended network on both sides, and treat unsolicited “bridge” links, fake RPC endpoints and look-alike explorers as phishing. A bridge error can permanently lose funds. This page does not name, rank or endorse any bridge.

How to evaluate a token on Solana

This is a reading list, not a trading method and not a reason to buy anything.

  • Read the mint, holders and transactions on the official explorer or Solscan.
  • Look at the pool and recent trades on a public screener such as DEX Screener.
  • Inspect holder clusters and linked wallets with Bubblemaps where the chain is covered.
  • Read spotting rugs for common failure patterns, and crypto research tools for a wider catalogue of free and paid research products.

None of those tools can make a token safe. They can only show you what is already on-chain.

Trading styles that fit Solana

This is a venue-fit note, not a recommendation to trade. Styles that are best suited to Solana, because official docs set a 5,000-lamport base fee per signature and 200 ms slots (as of the 9 Oct 2026 SIMD-0525 notes), and because Pump.fun, Raydium and Jupiter are commonly used venues, are short-horizon launch timing and DEX activity, plus ordinary DEX swings in more liquid Solana markets. A dated DefiLlama snapshot on 11 Nov 2026 (Asia/Calcutta) put Solana near the top of 24-hour DEX volume (~$1.56B). Centralized-exchange DCA or grid can still apply to majors; those styles do not use the chain’s slot speed. Main risks: rugs, bundles, honeypots, attention dumps, failed transactions and priority-fee spikes. MEV-style competition for inclusion exists here even though it is not the same as Ethereum’s public-mempool sandwich. Risks

  • New tokens are usually worthless. Bonding-curve and “fair launch” labels do not remove insider allocations, hidden seller wallets, or malicious programs.
  • Phishing is common. Fake explorers, fake “support” accounts, airdrop sites and look-alike RPC endpoints try to steal seed phrases or approvals.
  • Network congestion and priority fees. When demand spikes, inclusion can slow and priority fees can rise well above the tiny base fee.
  • Bridging risk. A wrong network, a fake front-end or a bridge exploit can lose funds with no recourse.
  • Software and validator risk. Like any chain, clients, programs and operational incidents can delay or halt activity.

Nothing on this page is financial advice, a recommendation to hold SOL, or a suggestion to launch or trade a token.
