Robinhood Chain is a public, Ethereum-compatible Layer 2 built on Arbitrum Dedicated Blockchains. Official docs say the network is live, uses ETH as gas, has mainnet chain ID 4663, posts data to Ethereum with blobs, and sequences transactions first-come, first-served. Official bridging docs describe a canonical Arbitrum bridge with deposits of about 10 minutes and withdrawals of about 7 days. This page is a plain-language snapshot of those official figures. It is not a reason to use Robinhood Chain, to open a Robinhood account, or to buy any token.

Last verified 10 Nov 2026 (Asia/Calcutta) against official Robinhood Chain documentation and Robinhood’s support article. Network details can change; check the official docs before you rely on a figure.

What Robinhood Chain is

Official docs describe Robinhood Chain as a permissionless, EVM-compatible Layer 2 on Ethereum, built on Arbitrum Dedicated Blockchains. Official materials say it is designed with tokenized real-world assets (including stock tokens and ETFs) in mind. That is a stated design goal, not an assessment of any token, and not a reason to buy a tokenized stock or anything else. Official Robinhood support also states that Robinhood Chain operates independently of the main Robinhood brokerage and crypto accounts.

The official mainnet chain ID is 4663 (testnet is 46630). Official docs say the chain uses Ethereum blobs for data availability, and that transaction ordering is first-come, first-served: arrival time at the sequencer decides order, and a higher fee cannot bump a later transaction ahead of an earlier one. Official docs also say “Robinhood Chain is live.” This page does not ask you to create a Robinhood account or to connect a wallet.

Gas token, fees and speed

The gas token is ETH. Official gas docs say a fee has two components that are bundled into the gas you pay: an L2 execution fee, and an L1 data fee for posting calldata to Ethereum, which varies with Ethereum congestion and with the size of the transaction. There is no single official USD fee this page can quote without going stale.

A current official block-time figure was not stated on the connecting and gas pages checked on 10 Nov 2026, so this page does not invent one. Official docs emphasize predictable FCFS ordering rather than a published slot time.

Official connecting docs list a rate-limited public RPC at https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com, and they recommend third-party providers for production traffic. This page does not ask you to sign up for a provider.

Official explorer

Official connecting docs and the Robinhood support article list robinhoodchain.blockscout.com as the mainnet explorer. You can look up a transaction, an address or a token without connecting a wallet. Bookmark official URLs from the docs. Do not trust an explorer or RPC from a DM or a look-alike domain, and never paste a seed phrase or private key into a website.

DEXs and memecoin launchpads

Official Robinhood Chain docs include an ecosystem table that lists Uniswap as a public DEX. The same official page says inclusion is not an endorsement, partnership or warranty by Robinhood, and this site does not endorse those venues either. Official docs do not name a single canonical memecoin launchpad, and this page does not invent one. A pool on Robinhood Chain is not evidence that a token is safe or worth anything. Most new tokens fail. See spotting rugs.

Bridging basics

This is a description of official documentation, not an instruction to move funds. If you do not need an asset on Robinhood Chain, you do not need to bridge.

Official bridging docs describe a canonical Arbitrum bridge as the trustless default between Ethereum and Robinhood Chain, plus several partner routes that can be faster and are a different trust model. Official figures for the canonical path:

  • Deposits (Ethereum → Robinhood Chain) typically confirm in about 10 minutes. Official docs say a failed L2 leg can often be redeemed for a time rather than being immediately lost; still, a fake front-end can lose funds.
  • Withdrawals (Robinhood Chain → Ethereum) are a three-step process with a 7-day challenge period, after which a claim transaction is required on Ethereum and incurs L1 gas.

If you ever use a bridge, start from official Robinhood Chain docs, confirm chain ID 4663 on Robinhood Chain and chain ID 1 on Ethereum, double-check the domain, and treat unsolicited bridge links as phishing.

How to evaluate a token on Robinhood Chain

This is a reading list, not a trading method and not a reason to buy anything. Coverage on a newer L2 can be uneven.

  • Read the contract, holders and transactions on the official explorer.
  • Look at the pool and recent trades on a public screener such as DEX Screener, if the pair is listed there.
  • Inspect holder clusters with Bubblemaps where the chain is covered.
  • Use GoPlus and Token Sniffer only if they actually support Robinhood Chain for that contract.
  • Read spotting rugs and crypto research tools.

None of those tools can make a token safe. A “stock token” or RWA label is not a guarantee of legal rights, backing or price.

Risks

  • Layer-2 trust assumptions. Sequencer liveness and a 7-day canonical withdrawal window are different from holding assets on Ethereum L1.
  • Tokenized real-world assets are still tokens. Official design goals around stocks or ETFs do not make a token safe, transferable in every jurisdiction, or worth its reference price.
  • Independence from the Robinhood app. Official support says this chain is not the same as a Robinhood brokerage balance. Confusing the two can lose funds.
  • New tokens are usually worthless.
  • Phishing. Prefer docs.robinhood.com/chain and the official explorer host, not a chat link. This page does not ask you to sign up for Robinhood.

Nothing on this page is financial advice, a recommendation to hold ETH on Robinhood Chain, or a suggestion to launch or trade a token.
